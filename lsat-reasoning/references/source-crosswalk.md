## Source-grounded construct

**SOURCE:** LSAC describes the LSAT as assessing critical reading, analytical reasoning, logical reasoning, and persuasive writing. Its reading-comprehension documentation emphasizes understanding and insight in lengthy, complex materials. The task-analysis source is used to inspect the skill demands beneath the test description.

## Assessment-versus-competency boundary

**SOURCE:** The LSAT is an admissions assessment used by law schools.  
**INFERENCE:** The textbook competency is a transferable reasoning capability: construct a recoverable understanding of dense material, model relationships among claims and reasons, evaluate alternatives, and communicate a defensible conclusion.  
**NOT CLAIMED:** Legal knowledge, legal practice, client judgment, bar eligibility, or law-school success as an outcome.

## Construct decomposition

1. Orient to purpose, genre, speaker, audience, and question demand.
2. Segment dense text and recover relevant locators.
3. Identify claims, reasons, evidence, assumptions, conditions, and implications.
4. Compare reasoning relationships and eliminate explanations that do not fit.
5. Evaluate alternative interpretations and answer choices against evidence.
6. State a concise conclusion with a visible warrant.
7. Write a persuasive response that remains faithful to the supplied record.

## Evidence and task model

**CROSSWALK:** Evidence should include a text map, evidence ledger, reasoning map, alternative interpretation, answer or conclusion rationale, and short persuasive response. Task families include dense passage analysis, logical relationship analysis, argument repair, comparison, and timed transfer.  
**EVALUATOR DESIGN:** Score evidence traceability, reasoning validity, relevance, alternative handling, and communication. Do not score the learner on guessing LSAT item keys alone.

## Textbook spine

`Orient → Segment → Map reasoning → Test alternatives → Decide under constraints → Write and audit → Transfer to legal / policy / research texts`

## Failure modes and constraints

- quote or passage fragment without context;
- answer choice selected without a reasoning record;
- conclusion treated as fact;
- ambiguity erased;
- test-prep trick substituted for transferable reasoning;
- protected LSAT material copied into a public challenge set.
