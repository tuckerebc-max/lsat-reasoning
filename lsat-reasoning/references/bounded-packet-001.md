## `OPT-66-LSAT` — Dense passage to warranted conclusion

**Learner target:** Separate what a passage states from what a reasoning map supports.

**Original practice passage:**

> A city library considers extending evening hours. Supporters point to attendance at two pilot branches. Critics note that the pilots were located near a university and that the city did not measure whether the added hours served residents who lacked daytime access. The proposal therefore has evidence of demand, but not yet evidence that the proposed schedule reaches the residents named in its justification.

**Learner task:** Segment the passage, identify the claim, reasons, missing evidence, and strongest alternative explanation. Write a two-sentence conclusion that states what the evidence supports and what it does not.

**Required artifact:** locator map, claim-reason-evidence-warrant table, alternative explanation, qualified conclusion.

**Nonmodel:** “The pilot proves residents need longer hours.”  
**Feedback move:** Ask “Which words establish the population claim, and where is that population measured?”

**Agent handoff:** Agent proposes a map and missing-evidence questions; learner verifies every entry against the passage.

**Exit test:** A new passage with the same structure but different topic. Full credit requires evidence traceability and a limitation, not a preferred answer.
