# `OPT-66-LSAT` textbook-skill architecture

**Architecture ID:** `F2-ARCH-66-LSAT-001`  
**Status:** `SPEC_DRAFT`  
**Promise:** Move from answer selection to recoverable, defensible reasoning about dense text and arguments.

## Learner change

Before: the learner searches for a trick, keyword, or answer choice.  
After: the learner states the task, maps the supplied text, traces reasoning, tests alternatives, explains a conclusion, and audits the evidence path.

## Progression

`Orient → Segment → Map → Test → Decide → Explain → Transfer`

## Unit map

| Unit | Work | Learner evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Purpose, question demand, and locator discipline | task orientation and passage map |
| 2 | Dense reading and structural noticing | evidence ledger and structure summary |
| 3 | Claims, reasons, assumptions, and implications | reasoning map |
| 4 | Alternative answers and argument evaluation | choice audit / counterreading |
| 5 | Persuasive writing under a defined audience | short argumentative response |
| 6 | Timed transfer and reflection | integrated reasoning packet |

## Human-plus-agent design

The agent may segment, restate, propose candidate reasoning maps, and surface ambiguity. The human verifies the supplied text, selects the supported path, and owns the conclusion. The handoff record must preserve passage locators, evidence, alternatives, and unresolved uncertainty.

## Evaluator seed

Dimensions: task orientation, evidence traceability, reasoning validity, alternative handling, conclusion fit, communication, and authorship / rights compliance. A correct answer without a reasoning record is incomplete.

## First drafting packet

Draft `LSAT-001` will use an original public-policy passage, a deliberately attractive but unsupported answer path, and a learner-produced reasoning map. It will calibrate against the inherited Close Reading and Argumentation skills without reproducing LSAC items.
